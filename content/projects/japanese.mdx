I study Japanese with Claude, and at the end of a session I'd ask it to export the day's
vocabulary as a CSV. 単語カード (*tango kādo*, "word cards") is where those CSVs go:
each import becomes a deck, and the app drills it.

## What it does

- **Study modes:** classic flip cards, multiple choice, and reverse (English → Japanese),
  with a hint that shows an example sentence in Japanese only.
- **AI enrichment on demand:** Claude generates example sentences, mnemonics, and related
  words for any card.
- **Spaced repetition:** a lightweight score per card that decays over time, so the
  words you're forgetting come back around.
- **Real Japanese text handling:** morphological analysis (kuromoji) and kana conversion
  (WanaKana), so typed answers and conjugations are graded properly instead of by exact match.

## What it taught me

After eight phases shipped with every test green, I sat down to actually study and hit a
batch of bugs in a single session. The retrospective boiled down to one sentence: *"done"
meant "tests pass on hand-crafted fixtures," never "works on the real 956-word corpus, on
my phone, looking like the wireframe."* About 40% of my verb deck turned out to be
inflected forms the data model never accounted for.

The fixes were mostly process: test fixtures sampled from the real data, a mobile
viewport in the end-to-end tests, and a visual pass against the wireframes before
anything counts as done.
